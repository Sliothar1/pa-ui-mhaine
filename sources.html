Sources and honesty notes

  • FitzPatrick, E. Landscapes of the Learned: Placemaking, Poetry and Patronage in Gaelic Ireland c. 1300–1600. (Source for the court at Pallas, Ó Longargáin at Ballynabanaba, and the Cahermacnaghten comparison.)
  • FitzPatrick, E. (2016) ‘The last kings of Ireland: material expressions of Gaelic lordship c.1300–1400 A.D.’ In K. Buchanan and L.H.S. Dean with M. Penman (eds), Medieval and early modern representations of authority in Scotland and the British Isles, 197–213. Routledge.
  • Curley, D. P. A multi-disciplinary study of lordly centres in the later medieval Uí Chellaig lordship of Uí Maine, c.1100–1600 AD. PhD thesis, NUI Galway.
  • O'Gorman, T. A History of Fohenagh. (Doon, Clonbrock, townlands and local tradition.)
  • Knott, E. (ed. and trans.) (1911) ‘Filidh Éireann go haointeach: William Ó Ceallaigh’s Christmas feast to the poets of Ireland, A.D. 1351’, Ériu 5, 50–69.
  • Bardic Poetry Database, Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies: poem 932, Filidh Éireann go haointeach.
  • Annals on CELT: Annals of the Four Masters (M1353.7; M1504.13–14; M1167; M1316), Annals of Inisfallen (AI962.3; AI1014.2), Annals of Tigernach (T1139.4). The full list per claim is in data/claims.json.
  • O’Donovan, J. (1843) The Tribes and Customs of Hy-Many. (Treated as a claim; the map outline is a sketch, not traced.)
  • Hymany Way leaflet (Aughrim). The 1603 date is the year of O'Sullivan Beare's march through Aughrim, not the date of publication, which is not yet recorded.

The old walkway line on the overview map is indicative only. The Battle of Athenry (1316) is now sourced to the Annals of the Four Masters and Annals of Connacht. Not yet sourced: the Land Commission period.

Some writers (DIB, Curley) place the 1351 feast at Galey on Lough Ree; this site follows FitzPatrick.
