Sources and honesty notes

  • FitzPatrick, E. (2023) Landscapes of the Learned: Placing Gaelic Literati in Irish Lordships, 1300–1600. Oxford: Oxford University Press (Medieval History and Archaeology). Source for the court at Pallas, the 1351 feast site at Doon Fort (pp. 147–149, reporting her 2016 field survey), Ó Longargáin at Ballynabanaba, and the Cahermacnaghten comparison.
  • FitzPatrick, E. (2016) [book chapter; separate from her 2016 field survey] ‘The last kings of Ireland: material expressions of Gaelic lordship c.1300–1400 A.D.’ In K. Buchanan and L.H.S. Dean with M. Penman (eds), Medieval and early modern representations of authority in Scotland and the British Isles, 197–213. Routledge.
  • Curley, D. P. (2022) A multi-disciplinary study of lordly centres in the later medieval Uí Chellaig lordship of Uí Maine, c.1100–1600 AD. PhD thesis, University of Galway (NUI Galway).
  • O'Gorman, T. A History of Fohenagh. (Doon, Clonbrock, townlands and local tradition.)
  • Knott, E. (ed. and trans.) (1911) ‘Filidh Éireann go haointeach: William Ó Ceallaigh’s Christmas feast to the poets of Ireland, A.D. 1351’, Ériu 5, 50–69.
  • Bardic Poetry Database, Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies: poem 932, Filidh Éireann go haointeach.
  • Annals on CELT: Annals of the Four Masters (M1353.7; M1504.13–14; M1167; M1316), Annals of Inisfallen (AI962.3; AI1014.2), Annals of Tigernach (T1139.4). The full list per claim is in data/claims.json.
  • O’Donovan, J. (1843) The Tribes and Customs of Hy-Many. (Treated as a claim; the map outline is a sketch, not traced.)
  • Hymany Way leaflet (Aughrim). The leaflet's "1603" is the year O'Sullivan Beare's march passed through Aughrim. It is not the Battle of Aughrim (1691), and it is not the leaflet's publication date, which is not yet recorded.

The old walkway line on the overview map is indicative only. The Battle of Athenry (1316): Annals of the Four Masters M1316 and Annals of Connacht 1316 (annal identified, entry not pinned). Not yet sourced: the Land Commission period.

Some writers (DIB, Curley) place the 1351 feast at Galey on Lough Ree; this site follows FitzPatrick.
