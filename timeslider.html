Uí Mhaine through time

Twelve stops, from Máine Mór to the end of the old order. Watch the kingdom grow and settle along the Suck and the Shannon.

Approximate / interpretation. The shaded shapes sketch how far the kingdom reached in each era. They are not surveyed lines. Boundary survey coming soon: Garry Lohan's corrected boundary will be added after his survey.
How to read this map · confidence legend
  • Well attested: solid outline; places named in the annals or still standing.
  • Interpretation: dashed outline; a reasoned sketch from Curley's study.
  • Claimed: hatched; tradition or the outer reach of a claim. For c. 1400 the solid core sits inside a hatched claimed band.
  • Hover or focus a shape for its source. Each card says whether the event is recorded in an annal or told as tradition.

The twelve stops

  1. Teacht Mháine Mhóir · Máine Mór comes west (c. 500)

    The kingdom's own story tells of Máine Mór leading his people west to settle beyond the Shannon, and of a victory at Claenloch that secured the land. This is told as tradition, not record.

    Reference: Origin tradition; Claenloch dated 538 in later tradition · Date: c. 500 · Evidence: local tradition · Status: tradition, not verifiable

  2. Na craobhacha ríoga · The ruling lines settle (c. 800)

    Several branches of the dynasty share and contest the kingship, and the kingdom takes shape across the Suck valley.

    Reference: King-lists and annal entries for Uí Maine kings, 8th–9th c. · Date: c. 800 · Evidence: later source · Status: synthesis, to confirm

  3. Bás Mhurchadha · Murchad, king of Uí Maine (960)

    The annals record the death of Murchad son of Áed, king of Uí Maine. In this era the ruling line came to be known as Uí Chellaig, after an earlier king named Cellach.

    Reference: Annals of the Four Masters 960 (Annals of Ulster and Inisfallen give 962) · Date: 960 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked (AFM 960 / AU 962)

  4. Tadhg Mór i gCluain Tarbh · Tadg Mór at Clontarf (1014)

    Tadg Mór Ua Cellaig fights beside Brian Bórama at Clontarf and is remembered among the fallen, a proud memory for the family.

    Reference: Annals of Ulster 1014 · Date: 1014 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked

  5. Aibhneacha mar bhóithre · Rivers as roads (c. 1139)

    In this age Toirrdelbach Ó Conchobair, king of Connacht, is said to have worked on the waterways. Whatever the detail, boats on the Suck and Shannon tied Uí Maine to the wider midlands.

    Reference: Annal entry not yet found; date unconfirmed · Date: c. 1139 · Evidence: later source · Status: annal not found; date softened

  6. Teampall Uí Cheallaigh · A church at Clonmacnoise (1167)

    Conchobar Ua Ceallaigh and the people of Uí Maine build a church at Clonmacnoise, still remembered as O'Kelly's Church: a sign of the kingdom's close ties to the great monastery across the Shannon.

    Reference: Annals of the Four Masters 1167 · Date: 1167 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked (corrected from burial to church-building)

  7. Tadhg ag Baile Átha an Rí · Tadg at Athenry (1316)

    On 10 August 1316 Tadg Ó Ceallaigh, king of Uí Maine, falls at Athenry with many of his kin. The annals remember him as the man the Gaels hoped most from in his time. The lordship regroups around its core.

    Reference: Annals of the Four Masters and Annals of Connacht 1316 · Date: 1316 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked

  8. Nollaig na nÉigeas · The great Christmas feast (1351)

    Uilliam Buide Ó Ceallaigh welcomes the poets, judges, historians and musicians of Ireland for Christmas at his court at Pallas, and Gofraidh Fionn Ó Dálaigh praises it in verse.

    Reference: Annals of Clonmacnoise 1351; poem ed. Knott, Ériu 5 (1911) · Date: 1351 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked

  9. Mainistir Chill Chonaill · Kilconnell Friary (1353)

    Uilliam founds a Franciscan friary at Kilconnell, a stone centre of learning and prayer at the heart of the lordship.

    Reference: Annals of the Four Masters 1353 · Date: 1353 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked

  10. Uí Mhaine ina buaic · Widest extent (c. 1400)

    Around 1380 to 1420 the lordship is at its broadest, from Athlone and Roscommon to Abbeyknockmoy and Tiaquin, with Ballinasloe and Tycooly (Ticooly) inside it. The solid core is well attested; the hatched outer band is the claimed reach.

    Reference: Annal places c. 1380–1420, mapped by Curley · Date: c. 1400 · Evidence: later source · Status: interpretation (Curley)

  11. Cnoc Tuagh · Knockdoe (1504)

    A great battle near Knockdoe draws in lords from across Connacht and beyond, with Ó Ceallaigh at its heart.

    Reference: Annals of the Four Masters 1504 · Date: 1504 · Evidence: contemporary record · Status: checked

  12. Deireadh ré · The lordship ends (1590s–1600s)

    The lordship of Uí Mhaine ended with the Tudor changes of the late 1500s and the land settlements of the early 1600s. Its names, townlands, poems and stones remain, and that is what this site celebrates.

    Reference: Tudor and early Stuart settlements (Curley) · Date: 1590s–1600s · Evidence: later source · Status: summary
